\section{Lattice-Based Encryption within Hybrid-Quantum Supremacy}

Lattice-based encryption is a cornerstone of post-quantum cryptography, providing robust security against quantum computational threats. By integrating principles from Multiplicity Theory, this section outlines advancements in lattice cryptography, emphasizing prime-based encoding, recursive feedback mechanisms, and tensor network dynamics to enhance security and efficiency.

\subsection{Mathematical Framework}

A lattice \( \mathcal{L} \) in \( n \)-dimensional space is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \left\{ \mathbf{v} = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i \mathbf{b}_i \mid a_i \in \mathbb{Z} \right\},
\end{equation}
where \( \mathbf{b}_i \) are the basis vectors. To incorporate Multiplicity Theory, we encode basis vectors using primes:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{b}_i' = \mathbf{b}_i \cdot p_i, \quad p_i \in \text{Primes}.
\end{equation}
This encoding ensures modular arithmetic optimization and enhances randomness in lattice structure generation.

\subsection{Prime-Based Encoding for Key Generation}

Cryptographic keys in lattice-based systems are augmented with prime-based encoding:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{s}' = \mathbf{s} \odot P, \quad P = \text{diag}(p_1, p_2, \ldots, p_n),
\end{equation}
where \( \odot \) denotes element-wise multiplication. The public key is computed as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{A}' = \mathbf{A} \mod q,
\end{equation}
with \( q \) being a large prime for modular arithmetic.

\subsection{Recursive Feedback for Adaptive Lattice Structures}

A feedback function \( f(\mathcal{L}, t) \) dynamically refines the lattice:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{t+1} = f(\mathcal{L}_t, \mathbf{e}_t),
\end{equation}
where \( \mathbf{e}_t \) is the error vector introduced during encryption. This ensures minimal distortion of encrypted data:
\begin{equation}
    f(\mathcal{L}_t, \mathbf{e}_t) = \arg\min_{\mathcal{L}'} \|\mathbf{e}_t - \mathcal{L}'\|.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Tensor Networks for Multi-Dimensional Security}

The lattice structure is represented through tensor networks to capture multi-dimensional dependencies:
\begin{equation}
    T_{i_1 i_2 \cdots i_d} = \prod_{k=1}^d \phi(p_{i_k}),
\end{equation}
where \( \phi(p_{i_k}) \) encodes prime-related interactions. Tensor evolution follows:
\begin{equation}
    T(t+1) = T(t) \otimes F(t),
\end{equation}
with \( F(t) \) as a feedback operator ensuring system coherence.

\subsection{Unified Lattice Multiplicity Equation}

The dynamics of the lattice are captured by a unified equation integrating error correction and stochastic variability:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(t) = \sum_{i=1}^n \phi(p_i) \cdot \mathbf{b}_i(t) + \mathbf{e}(t) + T(t) \cdot F(t).
\end{equation}
\subsection{Decryption in the Lattice-Based Framework}

The decryption process in the lattice-based cryptographic framework aims to recover the original plaintext vector \( \mathbf{m} \) from the ciphertext \( \mathbf{c} \), utilizing the private key \( \mathbf{s} \) and the public key matrix \( \mathbf{A}' \). This process ensures the confidentiality of the transmitted data while maintaining robustness against both classical and quantum attacks.

\subsubsection{Mathematical Formulation}

Given the ciphertext:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{c} = \mathbf{A}' \mathbf{s}' + \mathbf{m} + \mathbf{e},
\end{equation}
where \( \mathbf{e} \) is the error vector, the decryption process involves reversing the encryption transformations.

To extract the plaintext, compute:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{m} = (\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{A}' \mathbf{s}') \mod q,
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \( \mathbf{A}' = \mathbf{A} \mod q \): Public key matrix.
    \item \( \mathbf{s}' = \mathbf{s} \odot P \): Prime-encoded private key, with \( P = \text{diag}(p_1, p_2, \ldots, p_n) \).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Error Correction}

The inclusion of the error vector \( \mathbf{e} \) ensures the ciphertext's indistinguishability. During decryption, the error is bounded such that:
\begin{equation}
    \|\mathbf{e}\| < \frac{q}{2},
\end{equation}
ensuring that the modulo operation correctly retrieves \( \mathbf{m} \) without ambiguity.

\subsubsection{Integration of Feedback Mechanisms}

To enhance decryption accuracy, a feedback mechanism adjusts the lattice basis dynamically. The feedback function \( f(\mathcal{L}, t) \) refines the lattice to minimize residual error:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{t+1} = f(\mathcal{L}_t, \mathbf{e}_t),
\end{equation}
where \( \mathbf{e}_t \) is the observed error at time \( t \). This recursive adjustment ensures robust recovery of plaintext under varying conditions.

\subsubsection{Error Resilience and Security}

The decryption process leverages Multiplicity Theory's stochastic and tensor-driven robustness to handle noise and adversarial conditions. By incorporating stochastic variability into the lattice:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{b}_i'' = \mathbf{b}_i' + \epsilon_i, \quad \epsilon_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2),
\end{equation}
the framework mitigates potential side-channel and quantum-based decryption attacks.

\subsubsection{Performance Metrics}

Decryption efficiency is evaluated based on:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Correctness:} Recovery of plaintext \( \mathbf{m} \) with minimal error.
    \item \textbf{Computational Complexity:} Time required for decryption compared to encryption.
    \item \textbf{Resilience:} Tolerance to quantum and classical attacks.
\end{itemize}

This structured approach to decryption ensures secure, efficient, and robust plaintext recovery, leveraging the advanced properties of lattice-based cryptography within the hybrid-quantum supremacy paradigm.
